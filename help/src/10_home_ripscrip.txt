@topic home_overview | START: the Active Desktop | START;Home;Active Desktop;desktop;Channel Bar;Active Channel;gleam;new channel content;Update Now;Active Desktop item;News Ticker;World News Ticker;Canada News Ticker;World News;starting Navigator;choosing a service | home
START, the first button on the ribbon, opens the Active Desktop, the service Navigator shows when it starts. Each of the other services has an icon on the desktop, in the order of the ribbon. Point at an icon to see what the service is.

To open a service, click its icon once, or move to it with the arrow keys and press ENTER. Each icon does the same as the service's button on the [[nav_ribbon|ribbon]].

The Channel Bar, on the right of the desktop when Navigator starts, shows the Active Channels you subscribe to. The Teletext is always first. To add a channel, click one of the dark gray spaces below it and type the channel's address, the address of its .cdf file. Click a channel to open it in the [[web_overview|Web]] service: its pages are listed in the Channels pane on the left, and the channel's own page opens on the right. The pane lists every channel you subscribe to, in the order of the Channel Bar. The open channel shows its logo with its pages below it; the others show their names. Point at a name to see that channel's logo, and click it to open that channel instead. Click the open channel's logo to go to its main page and close its list. Each page has its own small icon when the channel gives one, and a page icon when it does not. Click a page in the pane to open it; click the close box at the top of the pane to put it away. Point at a channel to read what it is about. To remove a channel you added, right-click it and choose Delete.

= Moving and Arranging the Channel Bar
Point near an edge of the Channel Bar to show its frame and the strip above it. Drag the strip to move the bar anywhere on the desktop, and drag the top or bottom edge to make it shorter or taller. When the channels do not all fit, an arrow appears at the end they run past: click it to see one more channel, or hold it down to keep going.

To change the order of your channels, drag a logo up or down the bar: a line shows where it will go, and it moves there when you let go. The Teletext always stays first. The Channels pane follows the same order.

The Channel Bar is always on the desktop: it cannot be closed or hidden. The arrow at the left of the strip opens [[home_customize|Customize My Desktop]].

= The Gleam
A small red corner with a white sparkle on a channel's logo is the gleam. It means the channel has new content since you last opened it. A small red sparkle appears on a page's icon in the Channels pane when that page has changed since you last read it, and on a channel's name in the pane when that channel has new content.

Navigator learns this from the channel file itself: each page listed in it carries the time it last changed, and Navigator compares that time with the one it remembers from your last visit. Opening the channel clears the gleam on its logo; opening a page from the Channels pane clears that page's gleam. Until then the gleams stay lit, even after you close and restart Navigator. A page that does not say when it changed, such as a live radio stream, never gleams. When you first add a channel nothing gleams: Navigator starts remembering from that moment. If a channel file gives no change times at all, Navigator watches the file and its pages for changes instead.

Navigator checks each channel on the channel's own schedule while it is running, never more often than once a minute. To check one at once, right-click it and choose Update Now.

= Active Desktop Items
An Active Desktop item is a small window of live content that sits on the desktop, under the icons. The Teletext's World News Ticker is there from the start: a new world news story every 15 seconds, from independent publishers, each credited to its source along the bottom of the ticker. Click the story to read the whole article in the [[web_overview|Web]] service.

Three more items of The Teletext's are ready but not shown at first: the Canada News Ticker, with The Teletext's own Canadian news, and maps of Montreal's weather and traffic. To show one, open [[home_customize|Customize My Desktop]] and check it. Click a map to open The Teletext's Weather or Traffic page. To add an item from elsewhere, click an "Add to Active Desktop" link on a web page. An item does not add a channel to the Channel Bar.

Point at an item to show its bar along the top. Drag the bar to move the item, and drag its lower-right corner to resize it if it can be resized. The menu button at the left of the bar opens the item in the Web service, refreshes it, or closes it. To show the World News Ticker again after closing it, right-click The Teletext on the Channel Bar and choose Show on Desktop, or use [[home_customize|Customize My Desktop]].

The Active Desktop has nothing to connect to. [[home_customize|Customize My Desktop]] chooses what it shows.

@topic home_customize | Customize My Desktop | Customize My Desktop;Active Desktop;Channel Bar;showing the Channel Bar;Active Desktop item;News Ticker;World News Ticker;Canada News Ticker;maps;Delete;Reset All | home
Customize My Desktop lists what can appear on the Active Desktop: the Channel Bar first, then each Active Desktop item by its name, such as the World News Ticker, the Canada News Ticker or a Montreal map. The Channel Bar is always shown, so its check box is gray and cannot be cleared. To open it, right-click an empty part of the desktop and choose Customize My Desktop, or choose it from the menu at the left of the Channel Bar's strip.

- Check an entry to show it on the desktop, and clear it to take it off. Choose **Apply** or **OK** for the change to take effect; **Cancel** leaves the desktop as it was.
- **Delete** removes the selected item for good, after asking. The Channel Bar cannot be deleted. To add a deleted item again, click its "Add to Active Desktop" link.
- **Reset All** puts the Channel Bar back in its first place and size and moves each item back to where a new item goes. Nothing is shown, hidden, added or deleted.

The Customize button in the "Add item to Active Desktop" box opens Customize My Desktop as well.

= About RIPscrip
RIPscrip, the Remote Imaging Protocol Script, was developed by TeleGrafix Communications in the early 1990s as a graphical presentation system for information services reached by modem. A text terminal could show letters and ANSI character graphics, but no point-and-click screens, and sending whole pictures over a telephone line was too slow.

RIPscrip solved this by sending short text commands that describe lines, boxes, circles, colours, text, icons and buttons. The terminal draws the screen itself, commonly in the 640 by 350, 16-colour {{gl_ega|EGA}} format of RIPterm 1.54. Newspapers and public services used it to put wire copy, local news, weather, notices and downloadable documents behind a graphical, mouse-driven screen. Sun.ONE, launched in March 1995 by The Gainesville Sun and the University of Florida College of Journalism and Communications, carried local, national and international news this way. Its time was short: web browsers soon reached everyone. Today active public RIPscrip services are exceptionally rare, and there is no registry from which they can be counted.

The Teletext's RIPscrip service implements the RIPscrip 1.54 command set, reconstructed from TeleGrafix's published specification. Its pictures are drawn at about the speed of a 14,400 bits per second modem; file downloads are not slowed.

= RIPscrip in Navigator
The RIPscrip service is a graphical terminal for {{gl_ripscrip|RIPscrip}} 1.54 bulletin boards, connected by {{gl_telnet|Telnet}}. You choose the board's buttons with the mouse or with their keys, and receive its files by {{gl_zmodem|Zmodem}}. It is set up for The Teletext's board, ripscrip.teletext.ca: news, markets and numbers, weather, traffic, public alerts and the print replica edition.

@topic rip_connect | Connecting to a RIPscrip Board | RIPscrip;connecting;Connect button;Disconnect;host;port;Telnet | rip
To connect:

1. Check the **Host** box. It starts with ripscrip.teletext.ca.
2. Check the **Port** box. It starts with 23, the {{gl_telnet|Telnet}} port.
3. Choose **Connect**.

The status bar shows "Connecting to" and the host. While the connection is being made the button reads Disconnect and is dimmed; it cannot be stopped, but it gives up after 20 seconds. When the board answers, the status bar shows "Connected to" and the host, the Host and Port boxes are locked, and the screen waits for the board's first picture.

Navigator answers the board's request for its RIPscrip version automatically, as a RIPscrip 1.54 terminal. It also reports its cursor position when the board asks, as an ANSI terminal does, so boards that first check for an ANSI terminal send their RIPscrip screens too. Plain text that a board sends between its pictures, such as a login prompt or a menu, appears in the RIPscrip text window, in the board's colours. A button that asks for it clears the screen before its command is sent, for example a Continue button that leads from a board's opening picture to its login prompt.

To disconnect, choose **Disconnect**. The status bar shows "Disconnected from" and the host, and the screen is cleared. If the board ends the session, the same thing happens. Navigator does not reconnect by itself; choose Connect again.

If you leave the RIPscrip service for another, the session stays open and the screen is kept.

@topic rip_settings | RIPscrip Settings | RIPscrip;settings;host;port | rip
The only settings are the **Host** and **Port** boxes, which can be changed while you are disconnected.

The RIPscrip service has no other options, and remembers nothing between sessions: the next time Navigator starts, Host and Port show ripscrip.teletext.ca and 23 again.

@topic rip_usage | Using a RIPscrip Board | RIPscrip;buttons;hotkeys;mouse;downloading files;Zmodem;Downloads directory | rip
= The screen
The board's 640 by 350 picture is shown at the proportions of a 4:3 monitor, as large as the window allows, with black bars at the sides or the top if needed. While a screen is being drawn the status bar shows how much has been read, and "Page: Done" when it is complete.

= Choosing a button
Click a button on the board's screen. It is shown pressed while you hold the mouse button, and its command is sent when you release it over the same button. To change your mind, move off the button before releasing it.

Many buttons also have a key, usually a letter or a digit shown on the button. Press the key and the button flashes, then its command is sent. Upper and lower case are the same.

When a screen has no buttons, what you type is sent to the board: letters, digits, SPACEBAR, ENTER, BACKSPACE and the hyphen. When a screen has buttons, only their keys work.

= Downloading a file
When you choose a download on the board, or the board starts sending a file, Navigator receives it by {{gl_zmodem|Zmodem}}. No dialog box appears:

- The file is saved in the Downloads directory set in Settings, Configuration. See [[nav_config|Configuration]].
- A file of the same name is never replaced: the new one is saved as name (1), name (2) and so on.
- The status bar shows the progress, with the progress bar when the size is known.
- Press ESC to cancel. A file that does not arrive completely is deleted.

While a file is being received, other keys and clicks are ignored. When it is done, the screen shows "Saved", the name and the size, and the status bar shows "Transfer: Done".

@topic rip_trouble | RIPscrip Troubleshooting | RIPscrip;troubleshooting;errors;Host not found;Connection refused;Connection timed out | rip
= "Enter a host name first."
The Host box is empty. Type a host name and choose Connect.

= "Connection error: Host not found"
The name in the Host box could not be found. Check its spelling; use the host's {{gl_dns|DNS name}}.

= "Connection error: Connection refused" or "Connection timed out"
The host did not accept a connection on that port. Check the Port box (23 for The Teletext's board), and that your network allows Telnet connections.

= The screen stays black after connecting
The status bar says the session is waiting for the RIPscrip scene: the board has not yet sent anything to show. Navigator shows a board's RIPscrip pictures and also the plain text it sends, such as a login prompt or a menu, so a black screen means the board has not answered yet. Wait a few seconds; if nothing appears, disconnect and check the Host and Port boxes. The Teletext's board at ripscrip.teletext.ca sends RIPscrip.

= Typing does nothing
On a screen with buttons, only the buttons' keys are sent. Click a button instead.

= "This screen has more than 128 clickable regions."
RIPscrip 1.54 allows 128 buttons on one screen. The board sent more; the extra ones do not respond.

= A download stopped
The status bar gives the reason, such as "The connection dropped during the transfer." or "The sender stopped responding." The partial file has been deleted; start the download again from the board.

= "Download held back"
The receiver could not start. Choose the download again; if it keeps happening, disconnect and connect again.
